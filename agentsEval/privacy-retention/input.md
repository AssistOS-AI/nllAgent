# Northstar Services Privacy Retention Register

## Document purpose

This register is a fictional evaluation document prepared for a deterministic privacy-retention audit. It describes a
small collection of record schedules maintained by Northstar Services. The surrounding prose provides realistic
context for reviewers, while the controlled declarations at the end of each record are the bounded statements that the
evaluation materializer accepts. Nothing here claims that Northstar is a real organization or that a cited obligation
is valid law. The audit asks only whether this document's explicit retention statements satisfy the accompanying
fictional authority.

Each schedule was reviewed by the privacy operations group during the same policy cycle. The group assigned a stable
record identifier and an assessment-scope identifier to prevent nearby prose from merging unrelated records. Category
labels are treated as source descriptions, not as proof that every data field in a production system has been
inventoried. The review notes repeatedly distinguish a closed exception search from an open one because absence is a
valid premise only in a bounded, exhaustively inspected scope.

## Schedule R1 — Support conversation transcripts

Customer support transcripts contain messages, contact details supplied during a request, routing metadata, and the
resolution recorded by an agent. Operations wants a long history for training and quality review. The privacy reviewer
found that those purposes did not themselves constitute a documented legal obligation. The exception-evidence folder
for this schedule was inspected in full and the reviewer recorded closure. No exception declaration appears for R1.
The controlled declaration therefore gives the evaluator a duration premise and a closed basis for determining that a
matching documented exception is absent.

RETENTION | id=R1 | category=support-transcript | years=7 | scope=scope-r1

COVERAGE | scope=scope-r1 | exceptions=closed

The seven-year number is an explicit register choice. It should not be replaced with a shorter operational aspiration
found elsewhere, and the closure line applies only to scope-r1. Reviewers expect the report to cite both controlled
lines if the duration is judged above the limit without an exception.

## Schedule R2 — Tax invoice archive

The invoice archive contains customer names, billing addresses, transaction references, and payment reconciliation
metadata. Finance selected an eight-year period. Unlike the support transcript schedule, this record has an exception
entry that is explicit, documented, and tied to the same stable identifier. The entry names its fictional authority and
states a review date. The scope was also checked completely, so the register presents no unresolved absence question.
The evaluation does not independently validate the cited legislation; it tests whether the documentary requirements
stated by the policy are present.

RETENTION | id=R2 | category=tax-invoice | years=8 | scope=scope-r2

EXCEPTION | retention=R2 | status=documented | authority=Fictional Tax Records Act section 41 | until=2032-12-31

COVERAGE | scope=scope-r2 | exceptions=closed

Finance notes that the review date is not a promise to delete automatically on that date. That operational nuance does
not alter the bounded exception test. The source anchors for the retention and exception declarations should appear in
an accepted-exception assessment.

## Schedule R3 — Customer profile preferences

Profile preferences contain a chosen display name, language, communication settings, and accessibility options. The
product team selected a five-year maximum and did not request a longer period. Exception evidence was inspected and the
scope was closed, although the comparison can be decided directly because the duration is exactly at the policy limit.
This schedule is the inclusive-boundary case: “more than five” must not accidentally be implemented as “five or more.”

RETENTION | id=R3 | category=customer-profile | years=5 | scope=scope-r3

COVERAGE | scope=scope-r3 | exceptions=closed

The register deliberately spells out the exact integer boundary. A compliant result should cite the retention line.
It need not pretend that a legal exception was required for an at-limit declaration.

## Schedule R4 — Research interview recordings

The research team keeps consented interview recordings and transcripts while a longitudinal study is active. It has
proposed nine years, but the legal review is not complete. The exception folder remains open because counsel has not
finished classifying several correspondence items. No controlled exception statement is present in the current
revision. The absence is therefore provisional. A reviewer must not convert “nothing has been entered yet” into proof
that no documented obligation exists.

RETENTION | id=R4 | category=research-interview | years=9 | scope=scope-r4

COVERAGE | scope=scope-r4 | exceptions=open

The expected result is driven by the open coverage boundary, not by sympathy for the research purpose. Later closure
could permit a violation finding if no exception is found, while a later documented exception could support acceptance.
This revision supplies neither final condition.

## Schedule R5 — Fraud investigation case files

Fraud case files include account identifiers, investigation correspondence, device observations, and analyst notes.
The schedule declares nine years. Two explicit exception statements were copied from separate review queues. One says
the obligation is documented and names a fictional authority; the other states that documentation is absent. The
register owner has not supplied a priority rule, withdrawal record, or reconciliation. Both statements refer to R5 and
must remain visible. Closed coverage confirms that the set was inspected, but closure cannot resolve incompatible
positive statements.

RETENTION | id=R5 | category=fraud-case | years=9 | scope=scope-r5

EXCEPTION | retention=R5 | status=documented | authority=Fictional Financial Integrity Code article 12 | until=2031-06-30

EXCEPTION | retention=R5 | status=undocumented | authority=none | until=unresolved

COVERAGE | scope=scope-r5 | exceptions=closed

The correct treatment is conflict, not acceptance based on the first line and not violation based on the second. Exact
evidence should include both incompatible exception anchors and the duration anchor.

## Schedule R6 — Marketing suppression list

The suppression list stores hashed contact points and a reason code so that opt-out choices can be honored. The policy
team has two explicit duration entries for the same record and scope: one draft says three years, while an approved
operations note says seven. The document offers no precedence rule and neither line is marked withdrawn. Closed
exception coverage does not solve the unstable comparison premise. Selecting whichever number produces the preferred
outcome would conceal the contradiction.

RETENTION | id=R6 | category=marketing-suppression | years=3 | scope=scope-r6

RETENTION | id=R6 | category=marketing-suppression | years=7 | scope=scope-r6

COVERAGE | scope=scope-r6 | exceptions=closed

This schedule checks that explicit source identity does not collapse conflicting declarations and that the report cites
both duration lines.

## Schedule R7 — Unmapped biometric template

A laboratory appendix mentions a newly proposed biometric template category that is not admitted by this experiment's
bounded category vocabulary. The line resembles a retention declaration, but its category is explicitly prefixed as
unmapped. The materializer must record an ontology gap and decline to fabricate a typed retention observation. The lack
of a typed term cannot count as compliance, violation, or non-applicability.

RETENTION | id=R7 | category=unmapped:biometric-template | years=6 | scope=scope-r7

COVERAGE | scope=scope-r7 | exceptions=closed

The appendix is included to demonstrate local incompleteness. Other well-typed schedules should still be assessed, but
the overall run should disclose that at least one relevant source line could not enter semantic state.

## Review notes and boundaries

This register intentionally contains no hidden exception for R1, no exception for R4, no reconciliation for R5, and no
priority rule for R6. Those absences are part of the fixture design. Only R1 has a closed scope that turns missing
exception evidence into a final negative premise. R4 remains open. R2 has positive documented evidence. R3 does not
need an exception because its duration is at the inclusive boundary.

The controlled declarations are not a universal policy language. They use one line per observation, stable identifiers,
whole-year durations, and explicit scope labels. Surrounding sentences help a human reviewer understand why the cases
exist, but the deterministic materializer does not infer additional durations or legal conclusions from free prose.
That bounded behavior is a limitation, not a claim that the prose has no meaning.

All expected assessments must be replayable from the accepted terms. Evidence anchors should quote the exact controlled
lines rather than this summary. Abstract range checks and concolic boundary generation are companion assurance
activities; concrete circuit execution remains the source of emitted results. Timings reported for this document are
measurements of the evaluation run and are not production service-level guarantees.

## Appendix A — Distributed review evidence

The annual review workbook is maintained separately from the schedule narratives, so this appendix repeats the
controlled review facts that matter across sections. The repetition is deliberate: a human auditor must reconcile the
stable record and scope identifiers instead of assuming that nearby prose supplies evidence. For R1 the review group
searched the exception folder associated with scope-r1, logged the completion, and found no controlled exception
statement. For R2 the documented tax-records entry remains linked to R2 even though its explanation appears earlier in
the invoice section. For R4 the search remains open, and the unresolved correspondence may not be converted into a
negative exception premise.

The workbook also notes that R5 arrived through two queues. Neither queue owns an authority-based precedence rule.
The documented line and the undocumented line therefore remain coexisting explicit observations. The closed status of
scope-r5 means that the bounded set was inspected; it does not erase the contradiction in that set. R6 has a different
problem: its exception search is closed, but its duration premise is inconsistent before the exception question can
decide the outcome.

Reviewers considered whether closure for scope-r2 could be reused for R1 because both schedules were examined during
the same meeting. They rejected that shortcut. Stable scope identity exists precisely to prevent organizational
proximity, reviewer identity, or similar labels from widening closure. The deterministic trace should make this
separation visible by attaching the scope-r1 coverage anchor only to R1's absence reasoning.

## Appendix B — Source and change-control notes

This revision has no explicit withdrawal instruction. Consequently, all controlled declarations shown above remain
active observations for the fixture. A later date, later heading, or phrase such as “approved operations note” is
background context and does not silently suppress another controlled line. That rule is particularly important for
R6: selecting one duration by order would turn a designed conflict into either satisfaction or violation.

The review team records the category vocabulary as intentionally bounded. Support transcripts, tax invoices, customer
profiles, research interviews, fraud cases, and marketing suppression entries are admitted. The biometric template is
not. The materialization program should emit a grounded ontology gap for R7 while continuing to materialize the other
records. The overall task may therefore be reported with limits even when six record-level findings were produced.

No prose paragraph in these appendices adds a new duration, exception, or closure observation. Coding-agent
materialization is expected to use human-language context to understand the document, but the accepted task program
must ground the precise semantic observations at the controlled lines. Findings are never written into LongTextJS.
They are derived later by the pinned circuit and must point back to those exact spans.

## Appendix C — Expected audit presentation

The business report should group each record assessment with its status and evidence. R1 requires its seven-year line
and closed-coverage line. R2 requires its eight-year line and documented exception line. R3 requires only the
five-year line. R4 requires its nine-year and open-coverage lines. R5 requires its nine-year line and both incompatible
exception lines. R6 requires both duration lines. R7 appears as a limitation rather than a fabricated assessment.

These presentation expectations do not authorize message-string matching as the semantic oracle. The executable
finding type, subject identity, evidence anchors, assurance, and trace remain the auditable result. Markdown is the
human view. The purpose of this medium-length register is to demonstrate that a compact task-local semantic program
can preserve long-range business distinctions without turning the document into an anonymous data object.
