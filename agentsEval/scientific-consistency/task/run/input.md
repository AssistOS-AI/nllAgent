# AURORA-24 internal integrated efficacy report

## Document control and study objective

This internal report summarizes the locked AURORA-24 study database for the sponsor's report-control meeting. The
study compared investigational therapy AX-17 with placebo in adults with persistent inflammatory symptoms. The report
is not a clinical recommendation. Its purpose is to reconcile the executive summary, analysis definitions, primary
results, subgroup exploration, tables, and conclusion before external drafting begins.

The primary objective was to estimate the week-24 difference between AX-17 and placebo in responder rate. A responder
had at least a 30% improvement from the subject's own baseline symptom score and no prohibited rescue treatment during
the week-24 assessment window. The protocol described a treatment-policy estimand for rescue treatment and treatment
discontinuation. The primary analysis used the modified intention-to-treat population and a model-adjusted response
rate difference expressed in percentage points.

## Executive summary

The executive summary states that AX-17 improved the primary responder outcome by 18% relative to placebo at week 24
in the modified intention-to-treat population. It labels the value “primary treatment benefit” and says that the
estimate is model adjusted. No baseline response rate or formula for a relative reduction appears in this section.

The same summary reports a 14.3 percentage-point adjusted difference in week-24 responder rate for AX-17 versus
placebo in the modified intention-to-treat population. It attributes this value to the treatment-policy estimand and
states that it is rounded to one decimal place. The summary also notes that the per-protocol sensitivity analysis
showed a 17.8 percentage-point difference, but it clearly labels that result as a sensitivity estimate in a different
analysis population.

For symptom severity, the summary says the mean improvement from each group's own baseline was 12.0 points with AX-17
and 8.1 points with placebo at week 12. Those are within-group mean changes, not a between-group response-rate metric.
The summary does not describe either change score as the primary treatment benefit.

## Analysis populations and estimands

The randomized population contained 428 participants: 215 assigned to AX-17 and 213 assigned to placebo. The safety
population contained all 425 participants who received at least one dose. The modified intention-to-treat population
contained 418 treated participants who had a baseline symptom score and at least one post-baseline assessment: 211 in
the AX-17 arm and 207 in the placebo arm.

The per-protocol population excluded major eligibility deviations, prohibited concomitant medication, and absence of
the week-24 assessment. It contained 366 participants. Because these exclusions occur after randomization, values from
the per-protocol population are not interchangeable with modified intention-to-treat estimates. The subgroup of
participants younger than 50 years contained 184 modified intention-to-treat participants and was exploratory.

The primary estimand was the treatment-policy difference in week-24 responder rate. Rescue use and discontinuation
were handled through multiple imputation under the treatment-policy strategy. The estimand was evaluated in the
modified intention-to-treat population. A hypothetical no-rescue estimand was used only in sensitivity analysis and
must not be treated as the primary estimand.

## Statistical methods

The primary analysis estimated adjusted responder rates using a generalized linear model with treatment, region, and
baseline severity stratum. The reported treatment contrast was AX-17 minus placebo, expressed as an absolute
percentage-point difference. Model-adjusted arm rates and their difference were calculated from marginal predictions.
The report prespecified one-decimal-place presentation in narrative sections, while the main table retained three
decimal places as proportions.

For the primary metric, a tabulated difference of 0.143 is therefore the same value as a narrative difference of 14.3
percentage points. It is not equivalent to a 14.3% relative improvement. A relative improvement would require an
explicit reference rate and a ratio-based formula, neither of which defines the primary metric.

The per-protocol sensitivity analysis used the same adjusted response-rate-difference metric and week-24 horizon but a
different analysis population. The no-rescue sensitivity analysis used the hypothetical estimand. The week-12 symptom
score analysis used arithmetic mean change from each group's own baseline. These analyses answer different questions
and should not be numerically reconciled with the primary response-rate difference.

The report considers a one-decimal narrative value consistent with a three-decimal proportion when the normalized
proportion lies within the rounding interval. Confidence intervals are reported separately from point estimates and
are not substituted for them.

## Primary efficacy results

In the modified intention-to-treat population at week 24, the model-adjusted responder rate was 0.614 for AX-17 and
0.471 for placebo. The adjusted absolute difference was 0.143, corresponding to 14.3 percentage points. The two-sided
95% confidence interval for the absolute difference was 0.051 to 0.235. This analysis used the treatment-policy
estimand described above.

A draft sentence remaining in the results narrative states that the adjusted absolute difference was 12.0 percentage
points for the same week-24 modified intention-to-treat treatment-policy analysis. The sentence cites the primary
model and does not name a different baseline, population, aggregation, horizon, estimand, or rounding rule. No table
or derivation supports 12.0 percentage points for this analysis target.

The unadjusted observed-case responder rates were 0.602 and 0.478, an unadjusted difference of 12.4 percentage points.
This observed-case estimate has a different aggregation and missing-data treatment from the adjusted primary result.
It is presented as descriptive and is not the primary adjusted estimate.

## Sensitivity and subgroup results

The per-protocol week-24 adjusted responder-rate difference was 17.8 percentage points for AX-17 minus placebo. The
population consisted only of the 366 per-protocol participants. This value is larger than the primary 14.3
percentage-point estimate, but the population definition differs and the report labels the analysis as sensitivity.

Under the hypothetical no-rescue estimand in the modified intention-to-treat population, the adjusted week-24
responder-rate difference was 15.6 percentage points. The analysis changes the estimand rather than the metric or
population. It therefore must not be treated as a contradictory restatement of the treatment-policy result.

Among participants younger than 50 years, the exploratory adjusted week-24 responder-rate difference was 21.1
percentage points. The corresponding value among participants aged 50 years or older was 9.2 percentage points.
These subgroup results concern narrower populations and were not powered for a primary inference.

At week 12, mean symptom-score change from each group's own baseline was -12.0 points for AX-17 and -8.1 points for
placebo. A separate adjusted least-squares mean contrast was -3.6 score points. Neither the within-group change nor the
least-squares mean contrast is a week-24 responder-rate difference.

## Table 7. Primary adjusted responder analysis

Table 7 is titled “Modified intention-to-treat treatment-policy responder analysis at week 24.” Its rows report AX-17
adjusted response proportion 0.614, placebo adjusted response proportion 0.471, and AX-17-minus-placebo adjusted
difference 0.143. The table footnote states that proportions use marginal model predictions and that the difference
is an absolute contrast. The confidence interval row reports 0.051 to 0.235.

The table's population footnote defines modified intention-to-treat as all dosed randomized participants with baseline
and post-baseline data. The estimand footnote identifies treatment policy for rescue and discontinuation. The display
retains three decimal places; it does not use relative percent notation.

## Table 8. Sensitivity analyses

Table 8 reports 0.178 for the per-protocol adjusted absolute response-rate difference at week 24 and 0.156 for the
hypothetical no-rescue estimand in the modified intention-to-treat population. The caption identifies both rows as
sensitivity analyses. A third row reports the unadjusted observed-case difference of 0.124 and labels it descriptive.

These values share some dimensions with the primary analysis but not all of them. Table 8 does not purport to replace
the primary table and does not provide support for the draft 12.0 percentage-point primary sentence.

## Data-quality appendix

The data-management reconciliation found no duplicate randomized identifiers. Three treated participants lacked any
post-baseline symptom assessment and four lacked the baseline score required by the modified intention-to-treat
definition. These facts explain the difference between randomized and modified intention-to-treat counts. They do not
change the analysis target after database lock.

The report-control extraction covered the executive summary, analysis-population definitions, estimand definition,
statistical methods, primary results, sensitivity results, subgroup section, Table 7, Table 8, and this appendix for
quantitative efficacy claims. Coverage is closed for primary and sensitivity efficacy claims within these sections.
Safety narratives and exploratory pharmacokinetic listings were outside this audit scope.

One editorial note asks whether “18% relative to placebo” in the executive summary was intended as a relative metric
or as informal wording for the 14.3 percentage-point primary difference. The source does not resolve that question.
Both interpretations must remain visible: as written, the metric is relative percent and is not comparable with the
absolute primary difference; if an editor later changes the metric, that new revision must be analyzed separately.

## Integrated conclusion

The integrated conclusion states that the primary week-24 modified intention-to-treat treatment-policy analysis showed
an adjusted absolute responder-rate difference of 14.3 percentage points for AX-17 versus placebo. It cites Table 7
and uses the same metric, baseline comparator, population, aggregation, horizon, and estimand as the primary result.

The conclusion separately notes that sensitivity estimates ranged from 15.6 to 17.8 percentage points under different
estimands or populations. It does not present those values as alternate calculations of the primary analysis. It also
describes the week-12 symptom-score findings as supportive but distinct.

Before external release, the report-control team must resolve the unsupported draft statement of 12.0 percentage
points for the primary analysis. The team must also clarify or remove the executive-summary phrase “18% relative to
placebo,” because it names a different metric and lacks the baseline required to reconstruct a relative effect. These
editorial actions do not change the locked Table 7 value of 0.143 or its normalized presentation as 14.3 percentage
points.

## Report-control disposition log

The statistical lead confirmed that Table 7 is the locked source for the primary point estimate. The lead classified
the 12.0 percentage-point primary sentence as a transcription defect, not an alternative model result. The report
owner retained the sentence in this internal revision so the automated control could demonstrate that a genuine
cross-section conflict is detected before editorial correction. The owner also retained the 18% relative phrase
because its metric ambiguity requires a separate editorial decision rather than numeric reconciliation.

The quality reviewer recorded three expected outcomes. First, 0.143 in Table 7, 14.3 percentage points in the primary
results, 14.3 percentage points in the executive summary, and 14.3 percentage points in the integrated conclusion
should normalize to a consistent primary value. Second, the 12.0 percentage-point sentence has the same analysis
dimensions and should be reported as a numeric conflict. Third, 17.8 in the per-protocol sensitivity analysis, 15.6
under the hypothetical estimand, 12.4 for the unadjusted observed-case analysis, and subgroup estimates should not be
compared as contradictory primary values because at least one critical dimension differs.

The reviewer did not authorize conversion of the 18% relative phrase into an absolute percentage-point difference.
There is no explicit reference risk or calculation supporting that conversion. The phrase therefore remains a
different-metric comparison and the audit should classify it as non-applicable for direct numeric conflict review,
while retaining a clarification item for human follow-up.

All sections named in the data-quality appendix were available to the report-control process in this revision. The
coverage declaration applies only to efficacy claim support in those sections. It does not assert complete extraction
of safety events, pharmacokinetic observations, protocol deviations unrelated to analysis-set membership, or external
medical evidence. A later source revision must receive a new task identity and new spans even if most prose remains
unchanged.
